We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Equity Implications of Federal-Local Cost-Sharing in Flood Buyouts: A Game-Theoretic Analysis with Heterogeneous Homeowners" for consideration in Natural Hazards.

This manuscript develops a three-level Stackelberg game to analyze how federal-local cost-sharing arrangements in flood buyout programs generate inequitable outcomes across income groups. Our key contributions include:

1. Identification of three distinct mechanisms driving inequity in buyout programs: differential discount rates, tax-base preservation incentives, and participation thresholds.

2. Quantitative analysis demonstrating that the current FEMA 75/25 cost-sharing arrangement produces a relocation ratio gap of 0.26 between low- and high-income households.

3. Evaluation of alternative policy mechanisms showing that equity-weighted cost sharing can achieve comparable equity outcomes at 25% lower federal cost.

The numerical analysis covers 34,493 households across nine flood-prone US regions, and the model is validated against four major historical buyout programs with prediction errors of 11-16%.

We believe this work aligns well with Natural Hazards' scope on risk assessment, disaster response, and policy analysis. The findings have direct implications for improving equity in climate adaptation policy.

This manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. All authors have approved the manuscript and agree with its submission.
